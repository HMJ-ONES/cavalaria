How Cavalaria works

  • Every piece is a Cavalaria — a fusion of knight and pawn. Each one moves both ways: as a pawn (1 square forward, 2 forward on its first move, captures 1 square diagonally forward) and as a knight (2 squares in one direction and 1 to the side, jumping over pieces).
  • Win by wiping the enemy out. There are no kings and no checkmate — capture all of the other side's pieces to win. There is no promotion and no en passant.
  • The river blocks the way. Only a piece already in the water is slowed by it. On dry land you jump with full, regular Ls — you just cannot jump over the river. A forward jump that lands in the water stays a full L; one that would land past the water is blocked, and shrinks to a single diagonal step into the water instead. Once in the water, you can complete the L as long as you stay inside it; stepping out is a single diagonal step, and a step across the river's bend is not allowed. Backward jumps are always full. A rook or bishop slide stops at the first river square it meets.
  • Last unit standing gets stronger. When a side is down to its very last piece, its player picks an extra power for it: ♜ Rook (slides along ranks and files) or ♝ Bishop (slides along diagonals). It keeps its knight and pawn moves too.
  • Formations are real cavalry. 16 pieces a side, in three ranks. Roman — a solid block, 8 + 6 + 2. Mongolian — horns out wide, 6 + 6 + 4.
  • White always moves first, in every mode — even Random.
  • Pass. If a side has no legal move, the turn simply passes to the other side. You can also pass on purpose with the ⏭ Pass button — but if both sides pass in a row, it's a draw.
  • Random river, honestly random. One river square per file, on rank 4 or 5 — so the river is always one connected band across the board, never two squares stacked in the same file (that would trap a pawn trying to step out of the river). Files may share a rank.
  • Board size. The 12×12 board ("Grand") plays by the same rules with 28 pieces a side in four ranks and the river across ranks 6–7, one square per file. Pick the size above — it applies to every mode.
  • Moves always arrive. Every move sends the full board, the other side confirms it, and both sides compare notes every few seconds — if a move ever gets lost, the boards repair themselves.
